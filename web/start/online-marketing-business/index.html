Module 1: Start here: know who you're talking to

Lesson 1 of 49 · 3 min read

Get specific about who you're marketing to

Content made for everyone tends to land with no one. Before you write another post, get specific about the one kind of person you most want to reach. That choice shapes your tone, your platform and your topics far more than any posting trick will.

Start with a short customer profile. Skip the made-up name and the stock photo. You only need honest answers to three questions:

  • What is their main frustration right now, in plain words?
  • What have they already tried, and why didn't it work?
  • What would make them trust someone new enough to buy?

Make it specific enough to picture. Say you offer bookkeeping on the side. "Small business owners" is too broad to write for. "Hairdressers who rent a chair and dread tax season" gives you topics, a tone and an obvious place to find them.

Then check your guesses with real people. Message five past customers, or five people who fit the profile if you don't have customers yet, and ask what they were struggling with before they found you and what nearly stopped them from buying. A short call or a few messages is plenty. Write down their exact phrases. The way people describe their own problem is usually sharper than anything you'd come up with alone, and those phrases often turn into your best headlines and hooks.

Keep the profile where you'll see it. Before you publish anything, ask one question: would this person stop scrolling for it? If not, rewrite it or drop it.

Your step

Write a short profile of your ideal customer, then message five people who fit it and ask what they were struggling with.

Lesson 2 of 49 · 1 min read

Use your customers' words, not your jargon

People buy when they recognize their own problem in what you wrote. The industry terms you use every day, like "brand positioning" or "lead nurturing", often sound vague to the person you're trying to reach. They'd never type those words into a search bar.

Go where customers already describe their problem: reviews of your business and your competitors', comments, emails, direct messages and notes from sales calls. Copy the exact phrases into one document. Someone who writes "I never know what to post" is handing you a better headline than "content strategy support". Use those phrases word for word in your next headline, ad or bio, and keep the rest for later.

Your step

Read ten reviews or customer messages today and copy the exact phrases people use to describe their problem into one note.

Lesson 3 of 49 · 1 min read

Check competitors' comments for unanswered questions

Your competitors have already done some of your research for you. Under their posts sit the questions people keep asking, and some of those never get a good answer. Each one is an opening where you can become the person who explains it properly, without fighting for attention on topics everyone already covers.

Once a quarter, pick three competitors and scroll through their last month or two of posts. Skip the posts themselves and read the comments. Note every question that shows up more than once and gets a vague reply, or none at all. Those become your next topics. The point isn't to copy what they make. It's to find what they leave out.

Your step

Pick three competitors, read the comments on their recent posts, and list five questions nobody answers well.

Lesson 4 of 49 · 3 min read

Pick one channel and learn it properly

Trying to post everywhere at once usually means being average everywhere instead of good somewhere. Every platform has its own format, its own pace and its own idea of what a good post looks like. Learning one of them well takes longer than most people expect, so start with one.

Choose the platform where the customer in your profile already spends time, not the one you personally enjoy most. A few quick ways to find out:

  • Ask five customers where they go for advice or recommendations on your topic
  • Check where your competitors get real comments, not just likes
  • Search your main topic on each platform and see where the conversation is active

Then commit. Study how the best accounts in your space open their posts, how long their videos run and what they put in their captions. Post consistently and give it a few months before you judge. Growth on a new channel usually starts slow and uneven, and three weeks of numbers tells you almost nothing.

Only add a second platform once the first is bringing in steady results, like regular inquiries or sales, not just views. Even then, start by repurposing what already works on your main channel instead of building a separate stream of content from scratch.

Your step

Ask five customers which platform they turn to for advice on your topic, then commit to the one that comes up most.

Lesson 5 of 49 · 1 min read

Commit for three months and park the rest

Each platform rewards showing up often, in its own style. Spread yourself across five and you never post enough on any of them for the platform, or your audience, to notice. You end up busy everywhere with nothing to show for it.

Choose one or two platforms where your ideal customers already spend time, and commit to them for three months. Put the end date in your calendar so you're not tempted to judge after two weeks. Leave the other accounts dormant: claim your name so nobody else takes it, add a line in the bio pointing to where you're active, and don't post there until your main channels are running smoothly.

Your step

Write down the one or two platforms you'll focus on, put a three-month review date in your calendar, and pause the rest.

Lesson 6 of 49 · 1 min read

Make your bio match what you sell right now

When someone likes a post and taps through to your profile, your bio is often the first thing they read. If it still mentions last season's offer, or a link that goes nowhere, they leave confused instead of curious.

A good bio answers three things in a line or two: who you help, what you offer right now, and what to do next. For example: "Bookkeeping for hair and beauty pros. Tax-season packages open, book a free call below." Whenever you launch or change an offer, update your bio line, your link and your pinned post the same day. Then check all three on the first of every month, because a broken link is easy to miss and costly to leave.

Your step

Rewrite your bio today so it says who you help, what you offer right now, and the one action to take next.
