Lesson 1 of 49 · 3 min read

Pick one platform and a specific niche

Start with one platform and one clear niche. Trying to grow on four apps at once splits your time four ways, and you end up with a thin presence everywhere instead of a real audience anywhere.

Each platform rewards something different. Short-form video apps favor fast, hook-driven clips. Others reward longer, more polished videos, or written posts and photos. Pick the one whose format suits how you like to create and where the people you want to reach already spend their time. If you love talking on camera for ten minutes, a short-clip app will feel like a fight. If you think in quick, punchy moments, it might be perfect.

Then narrow your niche. "Lifestyle" or "fitness" is too broad to stand out. "Strength training for busy parents" or "budget meal prep for students" tells a viewer in two seconds if this is for them. It also helps the platform work out who to show your posts to, and it lets brands see exactly who they'd reach by working with you.

Finally, choose something you'll still care about a year from now. An audience is built by showing up consistently for months, and real interest is what keeps you showing up when a video flops or life gets busy. A trending topic you're lukewarm about usually runs out of steam fast.

A quick test before you commit:

  • Can you list 30 post ideas in this niche without struggling?
  • Would you watch or read this kind of content yourself?
  • Can you describe your ideal viewer in one sentence?
Your step

Write one sentence naming your platform, niche and viewer, like "Short videos on budget meal prep for students."

Lesson 2 of 49 · 1 min read

Skip trends that don't fit your niche

Trends are tempting because they look like a shortcut to views. But your followers came for a specific topic, and a random dance or meme that has nothing to do with it confuses them. Even when it gets views, it tends to attract people who won't stick around for your normal posts.

Before you jump on a trend, ask one question: can I tie this clearly to my niche? A personal finance creator could use a trending sound to show three money mistakes. A dance with no link to money is a skip. If you can't make the connection in one sentence, let it pass and post something your core audience actually wants.

Your step

Next time a trend tempts you, write one sentence linking it to your niche. If you can't, skip it and post a core topic instead.

Lesson 3 of 49 · 3 min read

Learn the main ways creators get paid

There's no single way creators make money. Most creator businesses that last combine a few income sources, so it helps to know the options before you start building.

The main paths are:

  • Platform payouts: ad revenue sharing or creator funds, paid by the platform based on views
  • Brand deals: a company pays you to feature its product
  • Affiliate links: you earn a commission when someone buys through your link
  • Your own products: a digital guide, templates, a course or merch
  • Memberships: fans pay monthly for extra content or a community

Platform payouts feel like the obvious place to start, but they're rarely reliable on their own. Rates go up and down, eligibility rules change, and a platform can rewrite its monetization program with little notice. Treat them as a bonus, not a salary.

Brand deals are often the first real money a creator earns, and they can be good money. They depend on other companies' marketing budgets, though, and those get cut when times are tight.

Your own product tends to be the steadiest path over time, because nobody else controls it. It doesn't need to be big. Say you sell a $15 template pack, or a short guide that answers the question your audience asks most. That's a real start, and it works whether or not a brand is hiring this month.

You don't need all five at once. Pick one to focus on first and keep the others in mind as your audience grows.

Your step

List the five income paths and mark the one or two that fit your niche best. Note one small product your audience would want.

Lesson 4 of 49 · 3 min read

Build a content system so you never run dry

Waiting for inspiration before every post is a common reason creators post in bursts and then go quiet for two weeks. A simple system fixes that. It has three parts, and none of them needs fancy tools.

First, a running idea list. Ideas show up at random times: in the shower, mid-conversation, while you're scrolling. Capture every one in a single note on your phone. When it's time to create, you pick from a list instead of staring at a blank screen under deadline pressure.

Second, batching. Instead of making one piece a day, film or write several in one sitting. Setting up lights, getting camera-ready and getting into the right headspace all take time, and batching means you pay that cost once instead of five times.

Third, reuse what already worked. Your best past posts have proven that your audience wants them. Update them, retell them in a new format, or answer the follow-up questions people left in the comments. That keeps your calendar full without needing a brand-new idea every day.

Put together, a week might look like this:

  1. Monday: pick four ideas from your list and outline them
  2. Tuesday: film or write all four in one session
  3. Wednesday: edit and schedule them
  4. Rest of the week: post, reply to comments and add new ideas to the list

Adjust the days to fit your life. What matters is that creating becomes a routine, not a daily scramble.

Your step

Create a note called "Ideas" on your phone, add ten ideas now, and block a two-hour session this week to make three of them.

Lesson 5 of 49 · 1 min read

Keep one running list of content ideas

Good ideas rarely show up when you sit down to create. They arrive on a walk, in a comment or halfway through someone else's video, and they're gone an hour later if you don't write them down.

Keep one note on your phone just for ideas and add to it whenever something sparks, even if it's only three words. Comments and messages are full of ideas, because every question someone asks is a possible post. Once a week, read through the list, pick the strongest few and move them into your plan. That way every filming day starts with options instead of stress.

Your step

Make a phone note called "Ideas" and add five right now, including two questions your followers have asked you.

Lesson 6 of 49 · 1 min read

Plan next week in a simple content calendar

When you decide what to post the night before, you post whatever's easiest, not what's best. Planning a week ahead gives ideas time to develop and keeps you posting through busy weeks when you'd otherwise go quiet.

Your calendar doesn't need an app. Each weekend, open a simple table with three columns: date, topic and format. Fill it from your idea list, and only plan as many posts as you could realistically make in a busy week. Leave one slot open for something timely, like a question that takes off in your comments or news in your niche.

Your step

This weekend, make a table with date, topic and format columns, fill in next week's posts and leave one slot open.

Lesson 7 of 49 · 1 min read

Batch your filming into one or two sessions

Every time you create, there's setup: lights, camera, outfit, clearing the room and getting your head in the right place. Do that every day for a single post and it quietly eats hours each week.

Batching means paying that setup cost once. Pick one or two days a week and film or write four or five pieces back to back. Plan the topics the day before, with a few bullet points each, so you can move straight from one to the next. Change your shirt or background between takes if you want them to feel like different days. You finish with content ready to edit and schedule, and the rest of your week stays free.

Your step

Pick one day this week, choose four topics from your idea list tonight, and film or write all four back to back.
